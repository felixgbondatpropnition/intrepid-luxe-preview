Ten Days in India: Why I’d Go Back in a Heartbeat

I didn’t know what to expect from India.

Heading out with two friends for a ten-day trip around Delhi and Jaipur, I honestly did know what to expect.

From what I’d read in the media and heard from others, I was half expecting Delhi belly, dirt everywhere and a huge amount of poverty. Looking back, I realise how much those impressions had shaped my expectations before I’d even arrived.

What followed was the most incredible holiday, and an absolute eye-opener.

More than anything, it was the people who made it special. We met people from so many different backgrounds, walks of life and professions, and the hospitality was extraordinary. People were inviting, welcoming and generous with their time. I can’t tell you how much that warmth added to our trip.

We stayed in some beautiful properties and discovered accommodation to suit a whole range of budgets. I also visited properties run by some of my suppliers, including some of the most beautiful places I’ve ever seen. Those visits alone left me feeling inspired. Drinking a cool Mango Lassi on the lawn of the Rambagh Palace Hotel surrounded by some of their 300 peacocks is a wonderful memory that I will cherish for ever.

Our multiple daily auto-rickshaw rides became part of the fun. Getting around was incredibly inexpensive, and there was always something to catch our attention along the way. The colours, the street scenes, the sheer amount going on: every journey gave us something else to take in.

And then there was the food. We ate so well, from wonderfully affordable meals to visits to highly regarded restaurants where the bill was an absolute fraction of what we would have paid in London. Eating out was one of the great pleasures of the trip.

Riots were taking place in Delhi while we were there, and we spent time cocooned in our hotels and enjoying the neighbourhoods around us, away from the affected areas. I can only speak for our own experience: we felt safe where we were, while staying aware of what was happening and being sensible about where we went.

Delhi and Jaipur each left a very different impression.

Delhi was vast, but once we knew where we were heading, we found we could navigate it quite easily. Jaipur was absolutely crazy in the most wonderful sense. Cows wandered through the streets, camels appeared in the road, and we even saw elephants walking along. After a while, very little surprised us, yet we never stopped looking

One camel encounter made it onto my Instagram, and that post has now reached almost a million views. Clearly, I wasn’t the only person captivated by the sight!

We also spent hours in the wholesale fabric shops. The colours, patterns and choice were an absolute feast for the eyes. We found beautiful shirts and dresses for the equivalent of under £10, and browsing became a very happy way to lose track of time.

Ten days gave me a glimpse of India that was so much richer than the picture I’d built before arriving. I came home with memories of extraordinary hospitality, beautiful places, delicious food and so much laughter with my friends.

Would I go back to India?

In an absolute heartbeat.
