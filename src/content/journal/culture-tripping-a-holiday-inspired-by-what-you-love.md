As a child, I dreamt of going to Salzburg and recreating scenes from The Sound of Music. I wanted to see those gardens and mountain views for myself. I didn’t think of it as a cultural holiday, of course, but that’s exactly what I had in mind.

“Culture tripping” now has a name, although the idea is familiar: planning a holiday around an exhibition, a concert, a favourite film or an artist whose work you love.

It’s something travellers have done for centuries. The Grand Tour took visitors to Italy’s art and architecture in the 1700s and 1800s. Today, we follow artists’ footsteps, visit their homes and studios, or book a few days away to catch a particular exhibition.

Films and television can be just as persuasive. Sound of Music tours are part of Salzburg’s appeal, and at the Alcázar in Seville, I was struck by how many Game of Thrones fans had come to see the palace and gardens used as Dorne.

Sometimes, even a resemblance is enough. I always love visiting York, where visitors flock to the wonderfully Harry Potter-esque Shambles. The idea that it inspired Diagon Alley has stuck, despite J.K. Rowling saying she had never visited.

And then there’s live music. Whether it’s Taylor Swift, Céline Dion or a favourite band, a concert can be the reason to choose a city and make a holiday of it.

I like having that focus for a trip: something I’m looking forward to, with time around it to wander, eat well and see what else catches my interest. And Salzburg is still definitely on my list.
