I’m in my early fifties, I’ve spent years travelling, and I’ve never been on a cruise. I’ve thought about it many times, but I always come back to the same question. Would I enjoy being on a ship with that many people? With up to two days at sea at a stretch, would I settle into life on board or start to feel a little claustrophobic without being able to step ashore?

Then there are all the little things I’m curious about. How do drinks packages and tipping work? Which shore excursions are included? At dinner, will we have a table to ourselves or be seated with other guests? Does anyone still get invited to the captain’s table?

I’m also curious to see how much of the daily programme I’ll actually do. Will I come home a ballroom dancer, a bridge player, a golf pro or even a mixologist?

It’s taken a bit of planning, and hearing from the cruise team about the planned programme has made it all feel a little more real. This week I finally set sail, and I’m genuinely excited, especially about spending Thanksgiving at sea.

Cruising has such a loyal following. People go back year after year, and I’m curious to find out what keeps drawing them back. As an independent travel advisor, I want to experience it for myself so I can help my clients decide whether a cruise would suit them and what to look for when they book.

For now, I’m looking forward to waking up at sea for the first time. I’ll share what surprised me when I’m home. And who knows? By the time I step ashore, I might already be thinking about where I’d sail next.
