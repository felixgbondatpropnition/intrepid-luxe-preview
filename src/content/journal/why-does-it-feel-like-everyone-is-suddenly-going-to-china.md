I work with lots of specialist travel suppliers, and lately I’ve been struck by how many different ways they’re offering to see China. There’s *China by Rail*, *Iconic China*, *China by Private Jet* and *Walking the Great Wall of China*, to name a few. You could build a trip around the railways or the Great Wall, or make the hotels part of the experience with a stay at Four Seasons in Beijing or Mandarin Oriental in Shanghai.

At the same time, social media has changed what a holiday in China looks like. One minute you’re watching drones light up the sky over Chongqing, the next it’s giant pandas in Chengdu and someone lowering a slice of beef into a bubbling Sichuan hotpot. Suddenly, you can picture what you’d actually do there on a Tuesday.

And some of that curiosity seems to be turning into bookings. According to figures cited on *The Holiday Expert Show*, UK bookings to mainland China rose 97% year on year. Across five major European markets, bookings to Beijing rose 281% and Shanghai 163%. The figures don’t tell us how many people were booking to begin with, but it’s still a striking jump.

Could China give Japan a run for its money? I think so. If you like the idea of hopping on a fast train and spending a few days in a city where your next meal is half the fun, China has plenty to tempt you. Hotels and meals can be cheaper day to day, and direct flights from London have been advertised at under £300 return on selected dates.

**The only trouble with putting China on your list is deciding which China trip to do first.**
